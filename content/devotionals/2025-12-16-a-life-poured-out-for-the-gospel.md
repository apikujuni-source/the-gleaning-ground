**Scripture Reading:** Romans 15:14–33

> “I will not venture to speak of anything except what Christ has accomplished through me…” — Romans 15:18

As Apostle Paul concludes his letter, he reflects on the shape of his ministry. What we see is a portrait of a redeemed and dedicated servant, whose calling, ambition, and labor are entirely defined by Christ’s work in him. Today’s passage gives us a window into the heart of a man who has surrendered everything to the gospel. His words remind us that redemption does not end with personal transformation, it propels us into purposeful service. Paul models what it means to serve with humility, to labor with purpose, and to depend on the prayers of God’s people.

Here are three truths from today’s passage:

1. Grace Shapes Our Calling. Here, Paul gave us an opening into his ministry to the gentiles as commissioned by the grace of God. His calling is priestly; proclaiming the gospel so that those who believe become an offering pleasing to God. We see the grace of God selecting the most unqualified person and equipping him with a sacred assignment. Grace doesn’t just save us; it assigns us, empowers us, and ensures Christ receives the glory.

2. Christ Defines Our Ambition. Paul refuses to boast in anything except what Christ has accomplished through him: the obedience of the Gentiles, the signs and wonders, the spread of the gospel. He acknowledges that these are the Spirit’s enabled works and not his achievements. His ambition is clear: to preach Christ where He is not known. Redemption produces holy ambition in us.

3. The Gospel Unites Us. Paul shares his plans, his burdens, and his needs. He carries a financial gift from Gentile believers to Jewish believers. This is a beautiful picture of gospel-formed unity. He asks the Romans to join him in prayer: for protection, for favor, and for the joy of eventually visiting them. The Christian ministry is not done in solitary. It is strengthened by the prayers, generosity, and companionship of God’s people.

Today’s passage shows us what a life poured out to God looks like. It is humble: boasting only in Christ. It is purposeful: driven by a calling received from God. It is ambitious: longing to see Christ known where He is not yet named. And it is communal: dependent on the prayers and partnership of the church.

**Pray with me:** Dear Lord, thank You for redeeming me and calling me into Your service. Please purify my ambitions and motives, and teach me to depend on Your Spirit.
