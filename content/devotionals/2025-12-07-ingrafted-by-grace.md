**Scripture Reading:** Romans 11:1–24

> “If some of the branches have been broken off, and you, though a wild olive shoot, have been grafted in… do not consider yourself to be superior to those other branches.” Romans 11:17–18

In today’s passage, Paul explains how Israel’s stumbling leads to the inclusion of the Gentiles into the family tree of God. Their rejection opened the door for salvation to reach the Gentiles. But this is not the end of their story; Israel’s stumbling is not final. Using the image of an olive tree, Paul shows how Gentiles have been grafted into God’s covenant promises by faith. The olive tree serves as a powerful reminder that all believers, regardless of their background, depend on the same nourishing root of faith.

Here are three truths from today’s passage:

- Israel’s stumbling brought salvation to the world. Their rejection meant reconciliation for the Gentiles. Yet Paul anticipates a greater future—Israel’s full inclusion will bring even richer blessings. As we reflect on this divine mystery, we are reminded that God’s redemptive plan is not limited by human failure but moves forward in unexpected and gracious ways.

- Gentiles are grafted in by faith. Like wild olive shoots, Gentiles share in the nourishing root of God’s promises. This is not a cause for pride but for humility and gratitude, knowing we stand only by faith. As branches dependent on the root, we are encouraged to remain connected through trust and obedience.

- God’s kindness and sternness call us to reverence. His sternness is seen in judgment on unbelief, while His kindness is experienced in salvation. Both remind us to remain steadfast, humble, and dependent on His mercy, recognizing that our place in His family is not earned but given through grace.

Today’s passage reminds us that salvation is a story of mercy. Israel’s stumbling opened the door for Gentiles. This calls us to humility. A call to live each day aware of our dependence on God’s grace, welcoming others into the same hope we have received.

**Pray with me:** Lord, thank You for grafting me into Your covenant tree by faith. Please help me to be ever humble and grateful for Your kindness.
